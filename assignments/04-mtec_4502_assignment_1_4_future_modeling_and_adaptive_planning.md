## Part 3 – Prioritized Adaptive Action Plan (Informed by Future Modeling)

Move from gap identification to ordered execution.

First, classify your key gaps using the following expanded definitions:

### Foundational Gaps

Definition:
These are structural prerequisites. If unresolved, they limit access to multiple roles and slow all advancement.

Characteristics:

- Frequently required across entry and mid-level positions
- Enable other skills to function effectively
- Often tied to core technical or professional literacy

Examples:

- Programming fluency for interactive media roles
- Version control and collaborative workflow tools
- Portfolio development and documentation skills
- Fundamental audio engineering or design literacy

Diagnostic Test:
If I do not develop this skill, does my entire pathway narrow significantly?

---

### Differentiating Gaps

Definition:
These increase competitiveness within a chosen pathway. They are not always required, but they distinguish candidates.

Characteristics:

- Often tied to specialization
- Signal advanced capability or depth
- Improve hiring competitiveness

Examples:

- Advanced Unreal Engine optimization
- Spatial audio system integration
- Machine learning model deployment for creative tools
- UX research certification

Diagnostic Test:
Does this skill significantly increase my competitiveness within a defined role?

---

### Adjacent Gaps

Definition:
These expand flexibility and allow movement into hybrid or neighboring roles.

Characteristics:

- May not be required for your primary target role
- Increase career resilience
- Support role hybridization

Examples:

- Data visualization for a media technologist
- AI workflow design for a sound designer
- Systems thinking for a game developer

Diagnostic Test:
Does this skill expand my optionality or allow pivoting into adjacent roles?

---

Students should briefly justify why each major gap falls into its chosen category before moving to goal creation.

## Cross-Industry Examples of Gap Classification

Below are illustrative examples across different possible MTEC pathways.

### Example 1 – Immersive Experience Designer

Foundational Gap:

- Real-time engine proficiency (Unreal or Unity)
  → Without this, most immersive roles remain inaccessible.

Differentiating Gap:

- Advanced interaction design for XR environments
  → Not always required, but distinguishes high-level candidates.

Adjacent Gap:

- Basic machine learning integration for adaptive environments
  → Expands pathway into AI-augmented immersive systems.

---

### Example 2 – Game Audio Designer

Foundational Gap:

- Middleware implementation (Wwise / FMOD)
  → Core requirement across most job listings.

Differentiating Gap:

- Procedural audio system design
  → Signals advanced system-level thinking.

Adjacent Gap:

- Data analytics for player behavior interpretation
  → Enables movement toward interactive systems design roles.

---

### Example 3 – Creative Technologist

Foundational Gap:

- Programming fluency in Python or C++
  → Structural prerequisite for technical credibility.

Differentiating Gap:

- Deployment of machine learning models in production environments
  → Distinguishes applied AI capability.

Adjacent Gap:

- Product management or startup incubation literacy
  → Expands pathway into leadership or venture contexts.

---

### Example 4 – Media Producer / Project Lead

Foundational Gap:

- Project management and collaborative workflow systems
  → Required for almost all production roles.

Differentiating Gap:

- Advanced budgeting and funding acquisition skills
  → Improves competitiveness for higher-level roles.

Adjacent Gap:

- Technical literacy in immersive or AI systems
  → Allows transition into hybrid technical-director roles.

These examples are illustrative only. Your classification must reflect your specific pathway and be justified structurally, not aspirationally.
