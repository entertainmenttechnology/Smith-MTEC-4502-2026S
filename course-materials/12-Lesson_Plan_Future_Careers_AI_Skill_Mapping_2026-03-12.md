# MTEC 4502 - Career and Portfolio Seminar
## Lesson Plan: Future Careers Discussion, AI Connections, and Skill Mapping
**Date:** March 12, 2026  
**Instructor:** Dr. David B. Smith  
**Format:** In-person workshop and discussion  
**Duration:** 2 hours 30 minutes

## Session Focus
Students will expand possible future-facing roles in their fields, collaborate in a shared Google Doc, and use AI to map those roles to existing skill sets, transferable competencies, and priority development gaps.

## Learning Objectives
By the end of class, students will be able to:
1. Generate a broader list of plausible future and hybrid career roles in their domain.
2. Distinguish between current role requirements and likely future role evolution.
3. Use AI critically to propose connections between role requirements and their existing skills.
4. Identify foundational, differentiating, and adjacent skills to prioritize next.
5. Leave class with a documented draft they can develop into Assignment 1C Part 2 work.

## Pre-Class Setup (Instructor)
1. Create one shared Google Doc titled: "MTEC 4502 - Future Careers and AI Skill Mapping (March 12, 2026)".
2. Set sharing to "Anyone with the link can edit" (or CUNY domain edit).
3. Create separate tabs in the Google Doc before class:
  - Tab 1: Emerging and Hybrid Roles (Whole class)
  - Tab 2: Role-to-Skill Mapping (Small groups)
  - Individual tabs: one tab per student for Priority Skills and Adaptive Planning
  - Final tab: Exit Ticket and Monthly Review Tracker
4. Paste the relevant table templates below into the matching tab.
5. Pre-name the individual tabs with student names to reduce confusion and overwriting.
6. Prepare one AI tool option (ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, or Claude) and one backup option.

Tab rationale:
- Tabs will reduce scrolling and make it easier to move the whole class from shared brainstorming to group work to individual planning.
- Each tab should function as a separate activity space with a clear task and time block.
- Individual student tabs create clearer ownership and make it easier to revisit each student's planning over time.

Field guidance for Tab 1:

- Current Target Role: The job title you would apply for now or within 12 months.
  Include domain and level when possible (for example, "Entry-Level Game Audio Designer" or "Junior UX Researcher in EdTech").
- Emerging or Hybrid Role: A future-facing version of your path using Domain + Function + Context.
- Why this role may grow: 1-2 sentences with a concrete driver (hiring pattern, workflow change, regulation, platform shift).
- Signal Type: Choose one primary signal source: Hiring, Tool, or Industry.
- Evidence URL: Link to a credible source that supports the claim.

Quick quality check for Current Target Role:
- Too broad: "Designer" or "Creative Technologist"
- Better: "Junior Interaction Designer for Cultural Institutions"

Field guidance for Individual Student Tabs:
- Current Target Role: The realistic role you are preparing for first, based on current readiness.
  Include level, domain, and context (for example, "Junior Composer for Interactive Media" rather than "Composer").
- Window-aligned planning table:
  - Students must complete both rows so each skill set is explicitly tied to a time window.
  - In 1-2 years, define the first realistic landing role and skills needed to qualify.
  - In 3-5 years, define how the role evolves or hybridizes and what advanced skills become necessary.
- Constraints: Be specific (hours per week, software access, hardware limits, budget, collaboration access).
- Current skills I can transfer: List concrete skills, not traits.
  Better: "FMOD routing, motif development, DAW session organization".
  Not enough: "creative" or "hard-working".

Column descriptions for the Window-aligned planning table (use this order):
1. Updated Future Role: The refined role title after AI mapping, written as Domain + Function + Context.
2. Time Window: Use either 1-2 years (Near-term) or 3-5 years (Medium-term).
3. Role Focus: The main responsibilities or scope to prioritize in that window.
4. Priority Foundational Skill: One prerequisite skill required for baseline access to roles in that window.
5. Priority Differentiating Skill: One specialization skill that increases competitiveness in that window.
6. Priority Adjacent Skill: One optionality skill that supports pivoting or hybrid roles in that window.
7. Milestone or Portfolio Artifact: A concrete output that demonstrates progress (project, prototype, case study, certification).
8. First Action Step (2 weeks): The immediate next step that can be started now and completed in two weeks.
9. Evidence of Progress: What will verify advancement (artifact link, checkpoint, mentor feedback, completed module, documented test).

## Class Agenda (2h 30m)

### 1) Opening and Framing (15 min)
- Prompt: "Which jobs in your field feel stable, and which feel likely to transform?"
- Brief connection to Assignment 1C Part 2:
  - Future modeling
  - Adaptive planning
  - Skills stress-testing

### 2) Whole-Class Role Expansion (30 min)
- Students add at least 3 possible future/hybrid roles to Tab 1.
- Require each entry to include a short rationale plus at least one source URL.
- Require role names to be specific (Domain + Function + Context), not umbrella labels.
- Instructor facilitates pattern spotting:
  - Which roles are truly new?
  - Which are existing roles with AI-augmented workflows?

### 3) Small-Group AI Mapping Activity (40 min)
- Form groups of 3-4.
- Each group selects 2-3 roles from Tab 1.
- Groups use AI to complete Tab 2.
- Required AI input packet (beyond one row):
  - 2-3 selected role rows from Tab 1, including rationale and source URLs.
  - Student profile snapshot for the group: current skills, tools, and domain strengths.
  - Career time horizon: near-term (1-2 years) and medium-term (3-5 years).
  - Constraints: available time, budget, access to software/hardware, and portfolio goals.
  - Evidence set: at least 2 credible sources (reports, job postings, or industry analysis) pasted into the prompt context.
  - Output format request: return results as a table that matches Tab 2 columns.
- AI prompt starter (students can copy and adapt):
  "Using the roles, student skill profile, constraints, and sources below, project likely responsibilities by 2030, then map which existing skills transfer directly, which partially transfer, and which new skills are required. Label each recommendation as foundational, differentiating, or adjacent, explain why, and return a table aligned to our Tab 2 columns. For each major claim, cite which source supports it and label confidence as High, Medium, or Low."
- Verification rule:
  - Students must edit AI output with their own judgment.
  - Add a confidence label (High/Medium/Low).
  - Mark any claim that needs evidence.

### 4) Share-Out and Critical Review (25 min)
- Each group presents one role map in 2-3 minutes.
- Class discussion questions:
  - Which AI suggestions were most useful and why?
  - Which suggestions were too generic or inaccurate?
  - What human context did AI miss?

### 5) Individual Skill Prioritization Sprint (25 min)
- Students move to their own individual tab and complete their Adaptive Plan Snapshot.
- They must pick one skill in each category:
  - Foundational
  - Differentiating
  - Adjacent
- They define one concrete 2-week action and one measure of progress.

### 6) Wrap-Up and Exit Ticket (15 min)
- Exit ticket in Tab 4:
  1. One future role I now take seriously
  2. One current skill I can transfer immediately
  3. One skill gap I will prioritize first and why
  4. One AI output I accepted, revised, or rejected
- Introduce the ongoing process below and ask students to choose their first monthly review date.
- Instructor closes by linking today's work to next assignment deliverables.

## Continuous Future-Career Assessment Process (Use All Semester)
Students should revisit career assumptions regularly as tools, workflows, and hiring signals change.

### Monthly 5-Step Cycle (30-45 min)
1. Scan signals:
  - Find 3 new signals: one job posting pattern, one industry report/article, and one tool/platform update.
2. Update role map:
  - Revise one role title using Domain + Function + Context and note what changed.
3. Re-score skills:
  - Mark each priority skill as Growing, Stable, or Declining in market relevance.
4. Validate with AI and evidence:
  - Ask AI to propose implications, then verify each major claim with at least one credible source.
5. Decide one action:
  - Commit to one concrete next action for the next two weeks (project, tutorial, collaboration, or portfolio artifact).

### Quality Control Rules
- Do not accept AI forecasts without source checking.
- Distinguish hype from adoption evidence (who is hiring, paying, and deploying).
- Favor role evolution statements over replacement-only statements.
- Keep previous entries so students can see trajectory changes over time.

## Assessment for Today (Formative)
Students are considered on track if they complete:
1. At least 3 contributions in Template 1 (role expansion).
2. At least 1 group contribution in Template 2 (AI-mapped role analysis).
3. A completed entry in their own individual tab (adaptive plan snapshot).
4. Exit ticket responses.

## Instructor Facilitation Notes
- Keep students focused on evidence-based forecasting, not pure speculation.
- Ask for explicit distinction between:
  - "AI replaces task" vs "AI augments worker"
- Push students to connect role projections to real skill pathways and near-term actions.
- Reinforce that AI outputs are drafts, not final authority.

## Suggested Follow-Up Homework (Optional)
Ask students to convert the work from their individual tab into a 250-300 word adaptive planning paragraph for Assignment 1C Part 2, citing at least one source used in class.
Ask students to complete their first Monthly 5-Step Cycle entry before next class.
